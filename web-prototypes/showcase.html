App showcase · screens from the running app

Showcase

Every screen below is the VocalVerse mobile app itself — captured from the running build at a 390×844 viewport (2× for retina), signed in with a real account and real seeded data, not re-drawn mock-ups. Nine screens cover the whole surface: the learning and scoring loop, the community and its composer, and the messaging and notes tools. The frame geometry is fixed — 433×882 body, 389.5×843.5 screen area, 55.75 corner radius — and each capture is scaled to fit it.

Captured 390×844 (2x) 5 sets · 15 screens +1 recorded walkthrough Source live app · Vite dev
1

Mobile · learning & scoring

Learning centre is the daily landing page: a greeting with this week's summary, a level badge carrying a 70 / 500 XP bar, a 12-day streak and a 30-day study heat-map, then four shortcuts — wordbook, reading room, community footprint and my pronunciation. Scoring report replays a single take as a 92.4 total split into pronunciation 93 · grammar 91 · fluency 88 · coverage 100%, followed by a score and a one-line note for every sentence. My pronunciation rolls that up over time: 82 / 76 / 86 across pronunciation, fluency and grammar, a seven-session bar chart, and the weak phonemes (/θ/ /ð/ /r/) that the word-level assessment keeps surfacing.

2

Mobile · sing along, scored line by line

Song library opens with the diagnosis from your last take (88.1 overall · pitch 93 · rhythm 91) and a coaching hint, then the catalogue grouped into all / popular / saved, each track badged with its best score. Ready to sing shows the whole lyric sheet, a reference melody to listen to first, and a three-minute recording budget with a live pitch guide. The result is where the feature actually pays off: one overall score split into pitch, rhythm and pronunciation, a D3 pitch-alignment chart over the whole take, and a row per line with its entry offset and score — and when too few lines were sung it refuses to give a total and tells you to transpose instead.

3

Mobile · reading, off the shelf

The reading line was missing from this page entirely. Bookshelf holds public-domain novels — Alice, Pride and Prejudice, The Wizard of Oz, Don Quixote — each with its cover and author. Book detail carries the facts that matter for a learner: level, chapter count, word count. The reader is the actual surface: chapter title, paragraph-set prose, and tap-a-word lookup that drops the word into your wordbook.

assets/app-shots/bookshelf.png assets/app-shots/book-detail.png assets/app-shots/reader.png
4

Mobile · community & posting

Feed mixes a recommended stream with three channels — news, teaching notes and overseas life; every card carries its author and level, a timestamp, an excerpt and like / comment / save counts. Post detail opens the full article with its author badge, cover image and comment count. Composer is the posting surface: a 280-character budget, the same three channels, image-or-video rules (nine images up to 20 MB each, or one MP4/WebM up to 64 MB, never both) and topic tags.

assets/app-shots/community.png assets/app-shots/post-detail.png assets/app-shots/compose.png
5

Mobile · messages & notes

Notifications splits into three real streams — messages, notices and follows — each entry showing the sender's level and time (a LV4 senior sharing reading notes, a LV1 teen asking how to practise shadowing). Direct message is the one-to-one thread those entries lead into, with the peer's level badge, bubbles and timestamps. Notes is the vocabulary notebook the app builds while you learn — filterable by all / speaking / reading / culture, each entry keeping the phrase (pick up, run out of, shadowing, procrastinate), its meaning, source and date.
